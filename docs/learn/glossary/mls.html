MLS — home-buying glossary

What it means

MLS stands for Multiple Listing Service — a private database maintained by real estate professionals that catalogs properties currently for sale in a given area. When a seller's agent lists a home, they enter it into the local MLS, making it visible to every other member agent and their buyer clients. There are hundreds of regional MLS systems across the country, each operated by a local Realtor association, rather than one single national database.

Why it matters

The MLS is the backbone of how homes get found and sold. When you search on Zillow, Realtor.com, or Redfin, those sites are largely pulling data from MLS feeds. However, there's often a delay — MLS data reaches those public sites anywhere from a few hours to a day later. Buyers working directly with a licensed agent get real-time MLS access, which matters in fast-moving markets where homes go under contract within hours of listing. Sellers benefit because an MLS listing puts their home in front of every active buyer's agent in the region simultaneously, maximizing exposure without requiring separate negotiations with dozens of brokers.

Example

A homeowner in Austin hires a listing agent and signs a listing agreement on a Monday morning. The agent enters the property into the Austin Board of Realtors MLS by noon. By that afternoon, every buyer's agent in the area can see the listing and schedule showings. By Tuesday, the details have synced to Zillow and other public portals. Without the MLS, the seller would need to contact each agent individually.

Common confusions

  • MLS vs. Zillow/Redfin: These consumer sites are not the MLS — they display MLS data but with a lag and occasional inaccuracies. The MLS itself is only directly accessible to licensed, dues-paying members.
  • One national MLS: Many buyers assume there's a single unified database. In reality, there are roughly 500+ regional MLSs in the U.S., so an agent licensed in Chicago isn't automatically pulling listings from Miami.
  • FSBO and the MLS: Homeowners selling "For Sale By Owner" are not automatically listed in the MLS. They must either hire a licensed agent or pay a flat-fee MLS service to get their property entered.
  • Coming Soon vs. Active: Some listings appear in the MLS with a "Coming Soon" status, which limits showings. Buyers sometimes see these on public sites and assume the home is available immediately — it isn't until the status changes to Active.

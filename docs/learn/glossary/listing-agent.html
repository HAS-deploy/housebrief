listing agent — home-buying glossary

What it means

A listing agent is the real estate agent who represents the seller in a home sale. They are hired by the homeowner to market the property, set a competitive asking price, negotiate offers, and guide the transaction through to closing. The listing agent owes their primary legal duty — called fiduciary duty — to the seller, meaning they work in the seller's best interest throughout the process.

The term "listing agent" comes from the fact that this agent creates and manages the official property listing, typically on the Multiple Listing Service (MLS), which makes the home visible to buyers and other agents.

Why it matters

Understanding who the listing agent represents helps you know whose side they're on. If you're a buyer touring a home and speaking with the listing agent, that agent is not working for you — they're working for the seller. Anything you share with them (such as your maximum budget or urgency to move) can legally be used to benefit the seller during negotiations. This is why buyers are generally advised to have their own agent, called a buyer's agent.

If you're a seller, choosing the right listing agent matters enormously. Their pricing strategy, marketing reach, negotiation skill, and local market knowledge directly affect how quickly your home sells and for how much. Listing agents typically earn a commission (a percentage of the sale price) paid at closing, so their fee is tied to getting you a successful sale.

Example

A homeowner in Austin decides to sell their house. They interview three agents and hire one to list the property. That agent runs a comparative market analysis, recommends a listing price of $485,000, photographs the home, writes the MLS description, and hosts open houses. When offers come in, the listing agent presents them to the seller and negotiates on their behalf.

Common confusions

  • Listing agent vs. buyer's agent: These are two different roles. The listing agent represents the seller; the buyer's agent represents the buyer. In some transactions, the same agent represents both sides — this is called dual agency, which is worth understanding because it limits how fully either party can be represented.
  • Listing agent vs. selling agent: Confusingly, "selling agent" is an older industry term that sometimes refers to the buyer's agent (the one who "sells" the buyer on the home and brings them to the table) — not the seller's agent. The cleaner, modern terms are listing agent and buyer's agent.
  • Listing agent vs. listing broker: A broker has additional licensing and can legally operate a real estate business. An agent works under a broker's supervision. The listing agent you deal with day-to-day may be an agent whose broker is technically the party of record on the contract.

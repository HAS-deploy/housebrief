DTI ratio — home-buying glossary

What it means

DTI, or debt-to-income ratio, is the percentage of your gross monthly income that goes toward paying debts. Lenders calculate it by dividing your total monthly debt payments by your gross (pre-tax) monthly income. There are two versions: front-end DTI, which covers only your projected housing costs (mortgage principal, interest, taxes, and insurance), and back-end DTI, which includes all monthly debt obligations — housing plus car loans, student loans, credit cards, and any other recurring payments.

Why it matters

DTI is one of the primary numbers lenders use to decide whether to approve your mortgage and at what interest rate. Most conventional loan programs want a back-end DTI at or below 43–45%, while many lenders prefer to see it under 36%. FHA loans can sometimes allow a DTI up to 50% with compensating factors like strong credit or significant cash reserves. A high DTI doesn't just risk rejection — it can push you into a higher interest rate tier or require a larger down payment to offset the perceived risk. If your DTI is borderline, paying down a credit card or car loan before applying can meaningfully shift your approval odds.

Example

Say your gross monthly income is $7,000. Your debts look like this:

  • Estimated new mortgage payment: $1,500
  • Car loan: $350
  • Student loan: $200
  • Minimum credit card payment: $100

Total monthly debt: $2,150 Back-end DTI: $2,150 ÷ $7,000 = 30.7%

That sits comfortably within most lenders' limits.

Common confusions

DTI vs. credit utilization: Credit utilization is the share of your available revolving credit you're using — it affects your credit score, not your DTI directly. However, high balances increase your minimum monthly payments, which does raise your DTI.

Gross vs. net income: DTI always uses gross (pre-tax) income, not your take-home pay. Using net income will make your DTI look worse than what the lender actually calculates.

Front-end vs. back-end: When a lender says "your DTI is too high," they almost always mean back-end DTI. Front-end DTI is a secondary check some lenders use, particularly for government-backed loans, but back-end is the figure that carries the most weight in the approval decision.

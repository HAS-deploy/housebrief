buyer's agent — home-buying glossary

What it means

A buyer's agent is a licensed real estate agent who represents the interests of the person purchasing a home. Their job is to help you find properties, evaluate them, write offers, negotiate price and terms, and guide you through closing. Crucially, their fiduciary duty runs to you, the buyer — meaning they're legally obligated to act in your best interest, keep your information confidential, and give you honest advice even when it's uncomfortable.

In most U.S. transactions, the buyer's agent has historically been paid through the seller's proceeds via a commission split arranged at listing. Following the 2024 NAR settlement, this arrangement has shifted: buyers now typically sign a written agreement with their agent upfront that spells out compensation before touring homes, and how (or whether) that fee is covered by the seller is negotiated deal by deal.

Why it matters

Without a buyer's agent, you're negotiating directly against the listing agent, who legally represents the seller. That agent isn't your advocate — they can't be. A buyer's agent levels the field. They know local comps, can spot red flags in disclosures, know which contingencies to include, and understand how to structure an offer that's competitive without overpaying. They also coordinate inspections, appraisals, lenders, and attorneys, keeping the transaction on track toward closing.

Example

You find a house listed at $450,000. Your buyer's agent pulls comparable sales, determines the home is priced $15,000 high, and advises you to offer $435,000 with an inspection contingency. When the inspection reveals an aging HVAC system, they negotiate a $5,000 seller credit. You close at $435,000 with that credit — an outcome far less likely if you'd gone directly through the listing agent.

Common confusions

Buyer's agent vs. listing agent (seller's agent): These are opposite roles. The listing agent markets the home and owes loyalty to the seller. Never assume the listing agent is neutral if you approach them without your own representation.

Buyer's agent vs. dual agent: A dual agent represents both buyer and seller in the same transaction. This is legal in many states but significantly limits the advice either party can receive, since the agent can't fully advocate for both sides simultaneously.

Buyer's agent vs. real estate broker: A broker holds a higher license than an agent and can operate independently. Agents work under a broker's supervision. Either can act as your buyer's representative — the title matters less than the representation agreement you sign.
